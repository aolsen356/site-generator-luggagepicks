How to Choose the Right Luggage

A complete guide to selecting luggage that fits your travel style, budget, and needs. Learn what features actually matter and which are just marketing hype.

Step 1: Determine Your Travel Style

Before comparing features, think about how you actually travel:

  • Trip length: Weekend trips need different bags than month-long adventures
  • Frequency: Weekly travelers need durable bags; annual vacationers can prioritize value
  • Destinations: Cities with smooth floors vs. cobblestone streets vs. dirt roads
  • Airlines: Do you fly budget carriers with strict limits or legacy airlines?
  • Carry-on preference: Do you always carry on, always check, or mix?

Step 2: Choose the Right Size

Step 3: Hardside vs. Softside

This is one of the most important decisions. Neither is universally better - it depends on your needs.

Hardside Luggage

Pros:

  • Better protection for fragile items (electronics, souvenirs)
  • Water-resistant exterior
  • Easier to clean (just wipe down)
  • Modern appearance

Cons:

  • Can crack under extreme pressure or impact
  • No exterior pockets for quick access
  • May be heavier
  • Doesn't compress to fit tight spaces

Softside Luggage

Pros:

  • More flexible - can squeeze into tight spaces
  • Often lighter weight
  • External pockets for easy access
  • Usually more expandable

Cons:

  • Less protection for fragile contents
  • Can absorb liquids if spilled
  • May stain more easily
  • Fabric can tear

Step 4: Wheel Configuration

4-Wheel Spinners

Advantages: Roll in any direction, upright position, no arm strain, glide easily on smooth floors

Disadvantages: Wheels protrude (take interior space), more fragile, may roll away on slopes, struggle on rough surfaces

Best for: Airport travel, smooth surfaces, heavy bags

2-Wheel Inline

Advantages: More durable wheels, handle rough terrain, recessed wheels save space, stable on inclines

Disadvantages: Must tilt to roll, more arm effort, less maneuverable in tight spaces

Best for: Cobblestone streets, outdoor travel, durability priority

Step 5: Key Features to Consider

Essential Features

  • TSA-Approved Lock: Required for US travel - allows TSA to open without breaking the lock
  • Telescoping Handle: Multiple height settings accommodate different users
  • Interior Organization: Compression straps, dividers, and mesh pockets
  • Warranty: At minimum, look for a 3-year warranty on defects

Nice-to-Have Features

  • Expansion Capability: Extra 2-3" when needed (great for return trips)
  • USB Charging Port: Convenient but not essential (battery pack does the same thing)
  • External Pockets: Quick access to tickets, documents, chargers
  • Wet/Dry Compartments: Separate dirty laundry or wet items

Don't Overpay For

  • Smart luggage features: GPS tracking, scales, and motors add weight and complexity
  • Removable batteries: Required on planes anyway; simpler to use a power bank
  • Premium branding: Some brands charge for name recognition rather than quality

Step 6: Set Your Budget

Under $100

Good for occasional travelers. Brands like Rockland, Amazon Basics, and Coolife offer decent quality. Expect 2-3 years of moderate use. Skip if you travel frequently.

$100-250

Sweet spot for most travelers. Brands like Samsonite, Travelpro, American Tourister, and Delsey offer durability and features. Good warranties, 5+ year lifespan with regular use.

$250-500

Premium territory. Brands like Briggs & Riley, Tumi, and Away offer superior construction, lifetime warranties, and premium materials. Worth it for frequent travelers who want a buy-it-once solution.

$500+

Luxury luggage. Rimowa, Louis Vuitton, and similar brands. Often more about status than function. Quality is excellent but incremental improvements over $250-500 range.

Step 7: Check the Warranty

Warranty quality varies dramatically:

  • Briggs & Riley: Lifetime warranty covering even airline damage
  • Samsonite: 10-year limited warranty on most bags
  • Travelpro: Lifetime limited warranty on premium lines
  • Budget brands: Often only 1-2 years, manufacturing defects only

Read warranty terms carefully. "Limited" often excludes wheels, handles, and airline damage - the parts most likely to fail.

Quick Decision Framework

Still not sure? Use this framework:

If you travel once or twice a year...

Get a budget-friendly set in the $100-150 range. American Tourister or Rockland are good choices. Don't overthink it.

If you travel monthly...

Invest in mid-range quality ($150-300). Travelpro Maxlite or Samsonite Freeform offer durability without premium prices. Consider a single versatile piece rather than a set.

If you travel weekly...

Go premium. Briggs & Riley's lifetime warranty means you'll never buy luggage again. The higher upfront cost pays off over thousands of trips.

If you mostly fly budget airlines...

Get a bag that definitely meets the smallest dimensions you'll encounter. Measure twice, buy once. Consider softside for flexibility.

If you do adventure travel...

Skip traditional spinners. Get a durable wheeled duffel from Osprey, Eagle Creek, or Patagonia that can handle rough conditions.
